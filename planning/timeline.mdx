One of the most important things your Joy Inc. coordinator does is build a detailed, realistic day-of timeline — a minute-by-minute schedule that accounts for every vendor, transition, and moment of your celebration. This document becomes the backbone of your entire wedding day.

## Why a Timeline Matters

A well-crafted timeline is what transforms a wedding day from a series of disconnected events into a seamless, flowing experience. Without one, vendors may arrive at the wrong time, photo sessions may run long and cut into cocktail hour, or the reception entrance may happen before the room is ready. Your timeline ensures that every professional on your team knows exactly where to be and when — and it gives you the freedom to be fully present without keeping one eye on the clock.

## What Goes Into Your Timeline

Your coordinator accounts for every layer of the day when building your timeline. No detail is too small.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Vendor Arrival and Setup Times">
    Every vendor — from the florist arranging centerpieces to the caterer prepping the kitchen — is given a specific arrival window. Your coordinator confirms these times in advance and builds them into the master schedule so setup happens in the right order without anyone getting in each other's way.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Ceremony Start and Duration">
    The ceremony anchor is one of the first things your coordinator establishes. From there, everything before and after is built around it. Your coordinator accounts for processional length, the ceremony itself, and recessional timing so the schedule stays accurate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="First Look and Photo Session Windows">
    If you're doing a first look or extended portraits before the ceremony, your coordinator works with your photographer to carve out the right amount of time — without rushing and without cutting into other moments.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cocktail Hour Flow">
    Cocktail hour is often when the couple is finishing photos while guests are mingling. Your coordinator ensures the bar and appetizers are ready on time, entertainment (if any) is cued up, and the transition into the reception space is smooth.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Reception Entrance, Toasts, and First Dances">
    These are the high-energy, high-emotion moments your guests will remember. Your coordinator sequences the grand entrance, coordinates with the DJ or band for music cues, and lines up toasts and first dances so everything flows naturally without awkward pauses.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Dinner and Formalities">
    Whether you're doing a plated dinner, buffet, or family-style service, your coordinator works with the catering team to time dinner service around formalities — so toasts don't interrupt a course and the dance floor opens at the right moment.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cake Cutting, Bouquet Toss, and Send-Off Timing">
    The final beats of your celebration are mapped out just as carefully as the first ones. Your coordinator cues the cake cutting, coordinates the bouquet toss, and ensures your send-off — whether it's sparklers, bubbles, or a classic exit — happens at exactly the right moment with everyone in place.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How Your Coordinator Builds It

Your coordinator begins the timeline-building process by gathering input directly from you — your priorities, your must-have moments, and any timing preferences you have. From there, they reach out to your vendors to confirm arrival times, setup windows, and any special logistical needs unique to your venue or team.

Every timeline includes intentional buffer time. Real weddings rarely run exactly to the minute, and your coordinator accounts for that reality by building in small cushions between events. This means that if something runs a few minutes long, it doesn't create a ripple effect that throws off the rest of the day.

Once the timeline is finalized, your coordinator distributes it to every vendor on your team. The timeline is a living document — as details change in the weeks leading up to your wedding, your coordinator updates it and ensures everyone has the most current version.

The following is an example of what a partial wedding day timeline might look like. Your actual timeline will be customized to your specific wedding, venue, and vendor team.

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| 12:00 PM | Florist arrives — ceremony and reception setup begins |
| 1:00 PM | Hair and makeup team arrives for wedding party |
| 2:30 PM | Photographer arrives — getting-ready portraits begin |
| 3:00 PM | Caterer arrives — kitchen and reception setup begins |
| 3:30 PM | DJ arrives — sound check and equipment setup |
| 4:00 PM | Wedding party finishes getting ready — first look |
| 4:30 PM | Wedding party portraits |
| 5:00 PM | Guests begin arriving — ceremony music begins |
| 5:30 PM | Ceremony begins |
| 6:00 PM | Ceremony concludes — cocktail hour begins |
| 7:00 PM | Guests transition to reception space |
| 7:15 PM | Grand entrance and first dance |
| 7:30 PM | Welcome toast — dinner service begins |
| 8:30 PM | Cake cutting |
| 9:00 PM | Open dancing — send-off at 10:30 PM |

<Tip>
  Share the final timeline with your wedding party and close family at least a week before the wedding so everyone knows where to be and when. A well-informed wedding party makes the whole day run more smoothly.
</Tip>
