## How to Prepare Your Vendor List

The foundation of great vendor coordination is a complete, organized vendor list. Before your wedding, take some time to compile a spreadsheet with the following information for every vendor you've booked:

- **Vendor name and company**
- **Contact name** (the specific person who will be on-site)
- **Phone number** (a direct mobile number, not just an office line)
- **Confirmed arrival time**
- **Any special instructions** (loading dock access, parking requirements, setup order, etc.)

<Tip>
  Share your completed vendor list with your coordinator at least two weeks before the wedding. The more lead time your coordinator has, the more thoroughly they can confirm details, resolve any conflicts, and ensure the master timeline reflects reality.
</Tip>

## Common Vendor Questions Your Coordinator Answers

Vendors often have practical questions about the venue and the day's logistics. Your coordinator is prepared to answer all of them — before the wedding day even begins.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Where should vendors park?">
    Your coordinator will obtain parking information directly from your venue during the planning phase and share it with all vendors in advance. This includes details about load-in zones, vendor-specific parking areas, and any restrictions on when or where large vehicles may park.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When can vendors begin setup?">
    Setup windows are confirmed with your venue and built directly into the master timeline. Every vendor is given a specific arrival and setup start time so that multiple vendors can move through the space efficiently without getting in each other's way.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who do vendors contact if they have a question on the wedding day?">
    Your coordinator. Before your wedding, every vendor receives your coordinator's direct phone number. On the day of, all vendor communication flows through the coordinator — not through you or your partner — so your experience is entirely celebration-focused from the moment you arrive.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if a vendor is late or has an issue?">
    Your coordinator is prepared for the unexpected. If a vendor is running behind schedule, has a logistical issue, or needs to make a last-minute change, your coordinator activates contingency plans and manages the situation directly. You stay in celebration mode while your coordinator handles the details behind the scenes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
