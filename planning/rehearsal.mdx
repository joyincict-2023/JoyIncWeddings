A rehearsal is your chance to walk through the ceremony with your wedding party before the big day. Your Joy Inc. coordinator facilitates the rehearsal, ensuring everyone knows their role, their position, and their cue — so that when the real moment arrives, it feels natural, calm, and exactly as you imagined.

## What Happens at the Rehearsal

Your coordinator takes the lead at the rehearsal so you don't have to. Here's how a typical rehearsal flows from start to finish:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Welcome and Introductions">
    Your coordinator greets the wedding party, officiant, and any family members present. They introduce themselves, give a brief overview of the plan for the rehearsal, and get everyone settled and focused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Processional Order and Timing">
    Your coordinator walks everyone through the processional — who walks with whom, in what order, and at what pace. They cue up the music timing (or work with whoever is managing music) so each person knows exactly when to begin their walk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ceremony Positions">
    Once the processional is complete, your coordinator directs each member of the wedding party to their ceremony position — ensuring the couple, wedding party, and officiant are all properly placed and that sightlines and spacing look right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recessional Walkthrough">
    Your coordinator guides everyone through the recessional — the joyful exit after the ceremony is complete. This includes the order of exit, pacing, and any special elements like an arch or aisle décor to be aware of.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Special Ceremony Elements">
    If your ceremony includes any special moments — a unity candle, ring warming, sand ceremony, hand-fasting, or family blessing — your coordinator walks through the logistics of each one so there are no surprises during the ceremony itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final Questions and Confirmations">
    Before wrapping up, your coordinator opens the floor for questions from the wedding party and family. They also confirm any final details — timing, transportation, and where everyone needs to be the following day — so everyone leaves feeling confident and prepared.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for a Smooth Rehearsal

A little preparation goes a long way. Keep these tips in mind as your rehearsal date approaches:

- **Arrive on time** — the rehearsal has a set window, and starting late cuts into practice time for everyone
- **Bring your full wedding party and immediate family** — if someone is walking in the processional or seated in a special row, they should be at the rehearsal
- **Have your ceremony music cues ready** — if you're using a playlist or working with a musician, confirm the song titles and timing so your coordinator can cue them properly
- **Bring a copy of your vows** — if anyone in the ceremony is doing a reading, having the text on hand avoids any last-minute scrambling
- **Confirm your officiant will be there** — rehearsals are most effective when the officiant can practice their role alongside the wedding party

<Note>
  If your venue requires a separate walkthrough or has specific rules about rehearsal timing, space, or access, let your coordinator know in advance so they can incorporate that information into the rehearsal plan and ensure everything goes smoothly.
</Note>
